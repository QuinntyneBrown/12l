12L design system

Ink, paper and one red L. Square corners, heavy numerals, lots of white space, one primary action per screen. Everything a 12L screen is made of, in one place.

Start here

The 12L design system is the marketplace's visual and interaction language written down once: the tokens, components and patterns every 12L screen is built from. It was extracted from the 124 mocks it cites (of the full set in docs/mocks), normalised into tokens, and verified for WCAG 2.2 AA in the light and dark themes.

Components

53 components in 7 groups. Every page shows anatomy, variants, sizes, the full state matrix in both themes, responsive rules, accessibility, content rules, do and don't, live tokens, code and the mocks it came from.

Actions

ButtonButtons start an action: buy, list an item, ship, save. One ink primary button per screen tells the member what matters most.Button groupA set of mutually related choices shown side by side: the condition picker on the sell form and the problem type in Report a problem are segmented groups.LinkLinks take the member somewhere: to the terms, a seller's profile, a tracking page. They are ink and underlined, so they never rely on colour alone.MenuA menu button opens a short list of actions or choices: Manage on a listing you own, More actions on a row, Sort on the catalog toolbar.

Inputs

CheckboxA square box for an independent yes/no: accepting terms, confirming the label shows US 12, picking any number of filters.File uploadThe photo uploader on the sell form and problem reports: a grid of square tiles, the first is the cover, with an Add photos tile that opens the file picker or takes a drop.Form fieldThe wrapper every input sits in: label, optional marker, control, help, error and counter, always in that order and spacing.Form layoutHow fields are arranged into a form: titled sections, two-up rows, the error summary at the top and an actions bar at the end that can stick to the bottom.Radio groupOne answer from a short list where every option should be read: a cancellation reason, a report decision, the shipping address at checkout.SelectA native drop-down for one answer from a fixed list: category, type, state, country, carrier, sort order.SwitchAn on/off control for a setting: which emails 12L sends, whether analytics cookies are allowed. The label names the setting, not the action.Text fieldA single-line input for short answers: email, display name, title, price, tracking number. Always inside a form field with a visible label.TextareaA multi-line input for descriptions, review replies, report details and notes. Grows with its content and shows a counter when there is a limit.

Navigation

BreadcrumbShows where a listing or order sits and lets the member step back up: Shop / Shoes / Sneakers.FooterThe quiet end of every member page: the small logo, the policy links and the copyright.PaginationMoves through long result sets. The catalog uses Load more; numbered pagination is the core alternative for tables and the audit log.Sidebar navigationA vertical list of grouped links for sections a member moves between often. Part of the core kit; no 12L mock uses it yet.Skip linkThe first thing a keyboard user reaches on every page: one link that jumps past the top bar to the main content.StepperShows progress through fixed stages. Orders use the timeline (Paid, Shipped, Delivered, Completed); the numbered stepper is the core form variant.TabsTabs switch between views of the same thing (listing statuses) or between sibling pages (the account sub-nav). The selected tab carries the red bar.Top barThe sticky header on every member screen: menu button, the 12L logo, the four category links, search, Sell and the member's avatar.

Containers and overlays

CardA bordered surface that groups related content: the order summary panel, the account stats, a payout account. 12L cards are square-cornered and mostly flat.DialogA modal window for one short task or one decision: add tracking, cancel a sale, withdraw a listing. On phones it rises from the bottom as a sheet.DividerA hairline that separates groups of content where white space alone is not enough: reviews in a dialog, order rows, form sections.Drawer and sheetPanels that slide in from an edge: the full-screen menu sheet on phones, the filters sheet, and a side drawer for previews.Layout primitivesContainer, page, stack, cluster, grid and split: the six classes every 12L screen is laid out with, driven by the layout tokens.TooltipA short label that appears on hover and focus to name an icon-only button. Core; the mocks rely on aria-label alone today.

Data display

AvatarA member's initials in a small square block: next to a seller's name, in the top bar as the account link, large on profiles.BadgeBadges and statuses label a record's state in a word: New seller, Hidden, Shipped, Sold. They are never buttons.ChipChips show the filters applied to the catalog and let the member remove one at a time, or toggle a quick filter on and off.Description listKey–value pairs for one record: a listing's facts, a member's account details, an order's tracking number.ListLists show one record per row: purchases and sales, listings, account settings, the moderation queue, the marketplace rules.StatA labelled number that summarises something: payouts paid and pending, a seller's sales and rating, what the seller earns after the fee.TableTables compare records across the same columns: the admin audit log, payout history, the accepted sizes in the marketplace rules.

Feedback

AlertAlerts put a lasting message where it applies: above a form after it failed, at the top of an order, or across the page as a banner when the whole site is affected.Cookie consentA small ink panel on a visitor's first visit: necessary cookies only until they choose, two equal choices and a link to the details.Empty stateWhat a list or area shows when it has nothing in it, nothing matches, or it could not load: an icon, a title, one sentence and one way forward.Error pageWhen a whole page cannot be shown: a big 12L-style code, a plain title, what is safe, and one way back. The shell stays so the member is never stranded.Inline messageSmall text next to the thing it is about: a field hint or error, a saved status, the reservation countdown, the secure-payment note.Progress barA thin bar that shows how far a measurable task has got: a photo upload, a data export.SkeletonGrey placeholders in the exact shape of the content that is loading, so the page does not jump when the data arrives.SpinnerA small turning ring for short waits of unknown length: checking an email link, loading an order confirmation, a button that is saving.ToastToasts confirm that something just happened (Listing published, Payment confirmed) or that a background request failed, without taking the member off the page.

12L brand

Buy barThe listing's purchase actions. On phones it sticks to the bottom of the screen with the total; from 1024px it sits in the info column.GalleryThe listing's photos: one large 4:5 image, a strip of square thumbnails to switch it, and position dots on phones.Listing cardOne item for sale in the catalog grid: photo with size tag, brand, title, condition and price. The whole card is one link to the listing.LogoThe 12L wordmark: two heavy ink numerals and a signal-red L with one 45° cut. It links home and is the only place the red appears at full size.Order summaryWhat is being bought or paid out, and the arithmetic: an item row, then label–amount rows ending in a ruled total.PhotoThe product photo frame: a 4:5 studio backdrop in one of six warm tones, the item centred, the size tag in the corner and a stamp when it is reserved or sold.PriceMoney, set in bold tabular figures. Whole dollars in the catalog, cents everywhere a member pays or gets paid.RatingSeller reputation as five stars, the average and the review count; and the star input buyers use to leave a review.ReviewA buyer's rating and words about a seller, with at most one public reply. The same quote and thread parts carry problem reports and cancellation requests.Size tagThe ink block that says US 12 or L. Every 12L listing is men's US 12 (shoes) or men's L (everything else); the tag carries that promise with the logo's 45° cut.

How to use

  1. Link the two stylesheets in this order; nothing else is needed for the components to render: 
    <link rel="stylesheet" href="docs/design-system/tokens/tokens.css">
<link rel="stylesheet" href="docs/design-system/assets/components.css">
  2. Build markup with the documented classes and native elements (<button class="btn btn--primary">, <div class="field">). States come from real attributes: disabled, aria-invalid, aria-busy, aria-pressed, aria-selected, aria-current, aria-expanded. data-state exists only so these pages can show hover and focus statically.
  3. Theme with data-theme="light|dark" on <html>; without it the OS preference applies. Never set colours in a component: override a component token (--btn-bg) or a semantic token instead. See Theming.
  4. Generate platform constants (Angular, .NET, email templates) from tokens/tokens.json, a W3C DTCG export with the dark theme under $extensions.
  5. Before shipping a new colour pairing, add it to tokens/contrast-pairs.json and run the contrast check.
